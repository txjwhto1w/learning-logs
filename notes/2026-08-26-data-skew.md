# Data Skew in Spark

Notes from debugging a slow join today.

## Symptom
- One executor takes much longer than others.
- Shuffle spill on a single task.

## Common causes
- Join key with high cardinality imbalance (e.g., `user_id` with a few hot users).
- Partitioning on a skewed column.

## Quick fixes
- Add salt to join keys: `concat(col, when(rand() < 0.1, rand()*10))` for the hot side.
- Use `skewed_join` hint in Spark 3.4+.
- Increase shuffle partitions, but that just spreads the skew, not solve it.

## Takeaway
Check `spark.taskMetrics` before optimizing. Always look at data distribution first.
